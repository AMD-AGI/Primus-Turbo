**Rulers**
1. **tune_attention.py** (09-13..09-16): one layer of fwd+bwd, median of CUDA events, iters 20, a 256 MiB L2 flush every time; four-tensor SQNR gate (out/dq/dk/dv ≥50 dB). Reports fwd / bwd / total ms; TF/s is computed on the fwd+bwd total.
2. **op-evolve benchmark.py**, per-call interleaving (the two A0 jobs 09-17..09-27; B0 fwd r5–r9; B0 bwd r24–r26):
   - Each arm takes the median of 51 calls, with palindromic interleaving; the ASM beat and the candidate run in the same process.
   - Three shapes: fast (1,1024,1024,8,2,128), proxy (1,4096,4096,32,8,128), prod (4,8192,8192,32,8,128); **ranking is by prod only**.
   - gain = the ratio of this round's code to the best-ever code, both re-measured in the same session. min_gain: for bwd it was 0 at first (changed to 0.007 on B0 on 09-28 and on A0 on 09-30); for fwd it is 0.007.
   - From r9 on, the fwd job no longer runs in the same process as the beat (h28). After r10 was rejected on a three-shape arithmetic mean of 1.0022, the operator added gain_weights (prod 1 / proxy 0.25 / fast 0), effective from r11 [a8fa47bf]. The A0 bwd job also added gain_weights from r25 on, and fast is now scored by min.
3. **Blocked timing** (blocked, h40/h41/h66): B0 fwd from the act of r10 on (the act was already timed with blocked 9 + lead 4 at 09-28 03:16 UTC [0927__b0/fwd/rounds/010/act.md], whereas ruler/REPORT.md records the harness swap at ~03:40 UTC); B0 bwd from r27 on (r27 in progress.md already reports a blocked beat of 817.8, whereas ruler/bwd/REPORT.md records the install time as ~05:00 UTC). In both places the recorded install time is later than the actual use; the round is authoritative. A0 has used it ever since. Each arm first runs 4 untimed calls, then 9 consecutive timed calls, with palindromic order between passes. An audit found that per-call interleaving pushes the FlyDSL/ASM ratio down: by ~25% for fwd and ~3.4% for bwd. After switching to blocked timing, the A/A error is ±0.19% for fwd and ±0.05% for bwd.
4. **Real data and the training operating point** (h47): the inputs are 6 layers of real q/k/v dumped at B0 step 43. "gb" means running 10 bf16 32768×4096×14336 GEMMs before each timing to pull the clock down to the training operating point (~1.28 GHz on A0). FlyDSL is clock-sensitive and ASM almost insensitive, so the FlyDSL/ASM ratio can differ by more than 25% between the randn/blk and real/gb conditions.
5. **e2e**: Llama-3.1-8B BF16, 32 layers (some 09-15/16 experiments used 8 layers), MBS=GBS=4, seq 8192, 32,768 tokens per step, single card, no activation checkpointing, no compile; tps = tokens/s.
   - From 09-16 on, attention comparisons presuppose nkfix (rewriting the GEMM layout of aten::mm at runtime).
   - NKFIX_CHECK=1 costs ~33 ms extra per step (it was on in the 10-02 e2e and turned off in the 10-05 tutorial).
   - torchtitan's mfu column is computed against the A100 peak and is unusable.

**FLOP convention**
- Current convention (op-evolve `tools/op_flops.py`): fwd 2.199292e12, bwd 5.498229e12 (5-GEMM nominal count, causal taken as (s+1)/(2s)), fwd+bwd 7.697e12; ms = FLOP / TF/s. Values marked derived in the tables are all converted this way.
- TF/s in the 09-13..09-16 documents is the fwd+bwd total (7.697e12 / total ms). The 0913/0914 documents also reported bwd on a 7-GEMM issued basis (e.g. B0 vendored fused bwd 748.4 vs 534.6 TF/s); this section does not use that convention. The 09-14 B0 Triton op-evolve job counts bwd only.
- The 09-23 STAGE2-FWD-SWEEP uses fwd ≈2.233e12 (old harness; TF/s ~1.5% too high). The 5.8 TF/s of 09-17 DKDV-FIRST-TIMING is on a custom "useful" convention (single head) and is not comparable.
- Our 1-wave bwd (r0 through s6) actually issues 7 GEMMs, 1.4076× the algorithmic amount; aiter ASM issues only 1.0155×. This section always counts the algorithmic amount. So s6's "103.8% of ASM" is achieved while doing ~39% more matrix math.

**"% of ASM"**: ASM time ÷ this row's time (equal to this row's TF/s ÷ ASM TF/s). Same-process or same-session ASM measurements are preferred; above 100% means faster than ASM. Before 09-14 there was no ASM yet; recorded as "—". op-evolve rounds use that round's same-session beat.

**Why numbers are not comparable across epochs (measured examples)**
- **Same code, same ruler, only the platform changed** (A0-T 09-28 → A0-R 09-30):

  - At the e2e level there is no clean like-for-like factor. The 1,947→1,351 ms (1.44×) for ASM attention + nkfix in tutorial §7 is not purely a platform difference: 1,947 comes from the void 09-28 a0_p3a process, in which the same-process FlyDSL arm was loaded from step 2 on and the fwd tree's `_env.py` then repointed `HIPBLASLT_TENSILE_LIBPATH` to the host library (whether hipBLASLt re-reads this variable mid-run is unproven); 1,351 is the 10-02 reading (NKFIX_CHECK=1 on both sides), and between the two runs the driver also changed from 7.1.1-2397345 to 7.1.0-2412954 (via -2410994, -2411826, -2411946) [0928__a0_repro/REPORT.md §2,§5.3,§6.3; 1002__e2e/E2E-PLAN.md §1].

- **Same code, only the inputs and operating point changed**: A0 10-02, bwd s6/ASM time ratio blk 0.972, gb 1.038; fwd r16/ASM blk 1.081, gb 1.349.
- **Values drift across an AC cycle**: A0 09-22, the same r7 code, 404.06 vs 382.73 TF/s (+5.6%). Same-session noise floor 0.24–0.66%, cross-session drift ~1.5%.

### c.4 Key points of the progress curve

- **The top e2e lever is GEMM, not attention.**
  - The hipBLASLt path fix took A0 32L from 244 to 2,027 tps (8.3×). nkfix then brought another 6.16× on A0 8L; on B0 32L the ASM arm reached 20,718 tps, 10.3× the pre-fix high tier of 2,016–2,022 tps (10.8× vs the low-tier median 1,922), with GEMM per step going 15.6–16.5 s→0.83–0.86 s, ~18–20×.
  - Before the fix, attention was only <1% to 3.3% of a step: on A0 09-13 32L via rocBLAS, the whole attention path was 35.7 ms × 32 = 1.14 s, ~0.85% (phase1/RESULTS.md:266); A0 8L via hipBLASLt without nkfix, 3.3% (BOTTLENECK-SHIFT); B0 09-28 before nkfix, 1.4–5.4% (0927__b0/e2e/RESULT.md:37). The e2e A/B of ASM bwd was therefore drowned in e2e noise (8L n=9, −0.28%, not significant). After the fix attention takes 15–22% (A0 8L 20.8%, B0 17–22%, A0-D 10-02 ~15%), and only then do op-level improvements become visible in e2e.
- **Triton stage: 2.44× in one day on A0, 2.75× in one day on B0.**
  - On A0-T 59.642→24.435 ms, from two config knobs plus the vendored aiter fused bwd.
  - On B0-14, first reproduced the Day-1 ladder (28.121→12.994 ms), then got down to 10.235 ms with waves_per_eu, ASM fwd (fwd 1.82×) and in-thread transpose: 2.75× vs factory default.
  - waves_per_eu shows no measurable gain on the throttled machine but is worth 4.2% at full clock: the machine state itself decides which levers work.
- **ASM fwd is the largest single jump in the fwd direction** (A0 4.253→1.549 ms, 2.74×) and has served as the bar ever since. The ASM bwd bar was at first overstated in time by 32% by a bogus 10.160 ms shim number (equivalent to understating ASM's speed); the 09-24 census set it at 7.6766 ms, and FlyDSL's "0.92×" was corrected to "0.70–0.72×" accordingly.
- **FlyDSL bwd from scratch to ~0.71×** (A0-T, 20 rounds, 57.24→511.42 TF/s, 8.9×; 0.714 vs the 09-24 bar of 716 TF/s, 0.72 using r22's same-session re-measurement of 516.87).
  - Comparing prod-champion TF/s round over round (each round's reading in its own session), the four biggest jumps are r1 1.62× (57.24→92.62, causal tile skipping), r4 1.60× (207.47→330.98, LDS padding removing 64-way bank conflicts), r2 1.55× (address clamping + 32-deep contraction + BLOCK_KV 32) and r3 1.45× (k_dq BLOCK_Q 64).
  - The framework's three-shape gain ranks them differently: r2 1.921, r4 1.512, r13 1.370, r1 1.342, r3 1.297. r13's gain came entirely from fast/proxy; prod did not move.
  - After r12 came the efficiency phase, with the ceiling capped by the 7 vs 5 GEMM structural factor of 1.386×; r13–r23 added only ~2–3% on prod.
- **fwd gains come mainly from scheduling and softmax instructions.**
  - On A0-T 935→1111 TF/s (+18.8%), made up of three items: longest-first scheduling, packed exp/row-sum, and nodelay.
  - On B0, speculative softmax gave +4.6% on randn but was actually slower on real data and at the training clock. After r16 turned speculation off (r13ns), real data got 8–15% faster, and real data plus the training clock 12–23% faster. The same change goes in opposite directions on the two rulers.
- **Corrections to rulers and criteria themselves "rewrote" part of the progress.**
  - After B0 switched from interleaved to blocked, fwd % of ASM went from ~85% to ~96%. At the same time two false wins were retracted (lab2-L12 +6.4%→−0.4%, lab3-L21 +6.3%→~+0.3%), and fwd r6's +5.7% was revised down to +4.6% (still a real gain).
  - r15's arithmetic mean, B0 r27's geomean falsely flagging target_met, and A0 r24's fast-median false positive: all three framework criterion errors were corrected by hand.
- **The A0 firmware reflash made the same code 1.24–1.67× faster (o1 order; in the reverse order o2, ASM fwd 1.23× and FlyDSL fwd 1.47×), with FlyDSL benefiting more**: bwd r29 rose from 69.5% to 83.6% of ASM, and fwd r13ns/ASM fell from 1.29 to 1.08. All A0 absolute numbers from before 09-29 are void. e2e has no clean same-convention ratio (the 1.44× is not pure, see c.1).
- **The bwd hand campaign went from 83.6% to 103.8% in one day** (09-30, r29 6.58→s6 5.295 ms, −19.5%).
  - The core lever is the 3-stage TDM LDS ring, plus reading the next iteration's B operands back into registers early. Both steps are whole-op changes: swapping in dkdv_tdm3 for k_dkdv alone made the whole op −11.17% vs r29 (5.856 vs 6.592 ms); after k_dqg also moved to TDM (s5), the whole op went a further −2.1% vs s4. Ablation A2 first gave the ceiling, then ATT (available only after the reflash) pinned it down to specific instructions.
  - s6 still has the 7-GEMM structure, doing ~39% more matrix math, and is still faster than ASM.
  - The 5-GEMM fused version w4f needs only 4.241 ms without dQ atomic accumulation (relax_noatom) (129.6% of ASM; output wrong, ceiling only), but dQ atomic writes eat ~5 ms, so it is paused.
- **e2e went from 4.8–5.0% behind to parity.**
  - On B0 fly/asm 1.0475 (r6+r20)→1.0454 (swap in fwd r13, still speculative)→1.0403 (swap in fwd r13ns)→1.0323 (swap in bwd r29). On A0-D, switching further to s6 gives 0.999, saving 53 ms of bwd per step. Absolute ms on B0 and A0-D are not comparable; only same-process ratios are compared here.
  - The remaining gap is in fwd: r16/ASM 1.349 at the training operating point, 9.2 ms slower per step.
  - The product branch is still on r29; s6 has not been ported into it yet. The same combination (fwd r16 + bwd r29) was 1.027× of ASM in #81, but that was a flydsl 0.3.4.1 build; the branch itself (0.2.4) has only been measured in the tutorial e2e at 1,361.9 ms/step, which is NKFIX_CHECK=0; converted to the same convention with +33 ms/step it is ~3% slower than ASM (estimated, not measured). Next steps are fwd, and switching the scoring ruler to the training-operating-point gb ruler (designed 10-02, not installed).
