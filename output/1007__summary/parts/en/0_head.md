# Llama-3.1-8B attention optimization on MI455X (gfx1250): progress summary (as of 2026-10-07)

- **Scope**: the attention used in Llama-3.1-8B training. Op shape b4 s8192 hq32 hkv8 d128 bf16, causal (bottom-right; equivalent to top-left when Sq=Skv), BSHD, GQA 4. The e2e setup is Llama-3.1-8B with 32 layers (some 09-15/16 experiments used 8 layers), BF16, single card, MBS=GBS=4, seq 8192 (32,768 tokens per step), AC none, torch.compile off.
- **FLOP convention**: the current convention is op-evolve's `tools/op_flops.py`: per call, fwd 2.199292e12 and bwd 5.498229e12 (5-GEMM nominal count), fwd+bwd 7.697e12. The 09-13..09-16 documents report only the fwd+bwd total (7.697e12 ÷ total time); this report does not mix in other conventions such as the 7-GEMM issued count or the old harness's fwd ≈2.233e12; see Appendix A.3.
- **Data cutoff**: the last measurement is the product-branch e2e tutorial of 2026-10-05 ((d) also records a wedge in another project on 10-06, as a risk reference only). This report was written on 2026-10-07 and revised once the same day after a completeness review (the pre-review full text is kept in `_work/SUMMARY.v1.md`).

## 0. One-page conclusions

1. **Starting point: unmodified main does not run on gfx1250 by default.** On upstream main `c1325c7e` (#481), with torch 2.11 it crashes on import (`register_opaque_type`); default dispatch lands on CK, which supports only CDNA (without aiter in the image, the very first step hits ImportError; with aiter installed, bwd fails mid-training with `invalid argument for fmha_bwd`); all tests on gfx1250 are skipped. The only backend that can be pinned by hand, Triton, copies CDNA's single config: on A0-VR, fwd+bwd 58.424 ms / 131.7 TF/s (bench0910, total convention; 59.642 ms / 129.0 on the branch tree's tune ruler), ~1.9× slower than flex, with the dkdv kernel alone taking 61.5%. On the e2e side, A0 had never completed a single step before; once it ran on 09-13 it reached only 245 tok/s, 133.7 s/step (GEMM fell back to rocBLAS, 27.4 TF/s), and `converters: []` meant that turbo attention never even entered e2e. Details in (a).
2. **Backends after adaptation (compared only within the same clock phase).** The A0-VR 09-13 bake-off (fwd+bwd total, fastest first): aiter Triton with 2 knobs 31.207 ms, flex 31.337, aiter Triton factory default 34.565, turbo Triton tuned 36.405, turbo Triton factory default 59.642, SDPA FLASH 97.063. The best Triton-family bwd takes about 2–2.4 times as long as ASM (≈, across rulers: the Triton figure is a reading from E1 `tune_attention.py`, whose timing includes the autograd plumbing; ASM is taken as the E3 benchmark.py bar of 7.68 ms, or as the slower same-process bring-up reading of 8.661 ms), and the best fwd about 1.5 times (≈, B0 09-14); this is a structural gap (a single ASM WG uses the full 320 KiB of LDS and 1024 VGPRs). aiter's precompiled ASM is the fastest ready-made implementation: on A0-VR, fwd 1.5724 ms / 1398.67 TF/s and bwd 7.6766 ms / ≈716 TF/s (09-24 census); after A0's firmware reflash, fwd 1.257 ms / 1750 and bwd 5.503 ms / 999 TF/s (09-30, blocked ruler, randn). Details in (b).
3. **The fastest implementation, ASM, cannot be used as is.** bwd has no Python entry point for gfx1250 (`can_impl_fmha_v3_bwd` does not recognize gfx1250), so a ~674-line launcher had to be written by hand; under GQA it writes dk/dv out of bounds per q head (at ratio=4, dk −0.94 / dv −0.65 dB); the workaround is `dkdv_heads=q` plus a host-side reduction, which costs 1.254 GiB more peak memory and 0.482 ms more in backward (A0-VR, +5.2%); only 52 `.co` files were released for gfx1250 (gfx950 has 1466), only 6 of them bwd, and no varlen; dq uses fp32 atomic accumulation, so results are nondeterministic; one call takes 3 kernels and 6 dispatches; aiter pins flydsl 0.3.2 and Primus-Turbo pins 0.2.4, so the two cannot share a process and ASM cannot become an upstream product backend. In addition, the ASM bwd 10.160 ms / 541 TF/s measured on 09-15 was an artifact of the autograd shim and was not retracted until 09-24; until then, FlyDSL's progress had been reported as 0.92× of ASM when it was actually 0.70–0.72×. These problems are the reasons for the 09-17 decision to develop in-house FlyDSL kernels.
4. **FlyDSL bwd: from 1/12.6 of ASM to 103.8% (the two endpoints lie in different firmware phases).** Hand-written from scratch on 09-17; on A0-VR, r0 ran at 96.05 ms / 57.24 TF/s (1/12.6 of same-session ASM); after 20 op-evolve rounds on A0-VR, r20 reached 511.42 TF/s (10.75 ms, ~0.71–0.72× of ASM); on B0, r29 (= r19h + u2n) reached 656.5 TF/s, 80.4% of ASM. The same r29 code on the same blocked ruler was only 69.5% of ASM on A0-VR (09-28), but 83.6% on A0 after the firmware reflash (09-30): estimated from r29, about 14 percentage points of the headline progress come from the reflash (FlyDSL is more clock-sensitive than ASM), not from the code. The 09-30 hand campaign s1–s6, built around a 3-stage TDM LDS ring plus reading the next iteration's B operand back into registers ahead of time, brought bwd to **5.295–5.300 ms / ≈1037–1038 TF/s, 103.8% of ASM (5.497–5.500 ms)** (A0 after the reflash, blocked ruler, randn, same process, bitwise deterministic). A 10-02 re-check with real data gave an s6/ASM time ratio of 0.972 on the blk ruler and 1.038 on the gb ruler at the training operating point. s6 still issues 7 GEMMs, doing ~39% more matrix math than ASM.
5. **FlyDSL fwd: still ~8% slower, and 35% slower at the training operating point.** The A0-VR fwd job went from 935.04 TF/s at r0 to 1111.15 TF/s at r11 (+18.8%; 78.9% of the same-session beat of 1407.8 TF/s, 79.4% against the bar of 1398.67 TF/s). On B0, speculative softmax was 4.6% faster on randn but actually slower with real data at the training clock; r16 turns speculation off (i.e., r13ns) and is 12–23% faster with real data plus the training clock. The current fwd champion is r16 = r13ns: on A0 after the reflash, measured on 09-30 at 1.353 ms / 1626 TF/s vs ASM 1.257 ms, a time ratio of 1.076 (92.9% of ASM); with real data on 10-02, blk 1.081 and gb **1.349**. fwd is now the main remaining gap.
6. **e2e: FlyDSL is now on par with ASM.** A0, 10-02 (driver 7.1.0-2412954, 32 layers, nkfix, `NKFIX_CHECK=1`, three arms alternating in the same process, median training sclk ~1.50 GHz): fwd r16 + bwd s6 at 1,349.4 / 1,349.9 ms per step and 24,283 / 24,275 tok/s, **0.999** of ASM (1,350.6 ms, 24,262 tok/s); fwd r16 + bwd r29 at 1.027. Split with CUDA events (kineto traces are invalid on A0): attention bwd is ~4 ms per step faster than ASM (adjacent-pair difference 4.5 ms; difference between the two arms' medians 3.8–4.4 ms), fwd is ~9.2 ms slower, so attention as a whole costs ~4.3–5.2 ms more, yet the step is 0.7–2.0 ms faster; this ~6 ms discrepancy (step − FA is −6.3 ms in both processes) lies in the parts outside attention or in timing noise and has not been broken down. Versus r29, s6 saves ~53 ms of attention bwd per step and the step is 37–39 ms faster, again with ~14–16 ms not broken down. Before that, the final B0 09-28 r16 + r29 stood at 1.0323 / 1.0328 (20,091 vs 20,747 tok/s). The product branch (r16 + r29, flydsl 0.2.4) measured 1,361.9 ms/step and 24,061 tok/s in the 10-05 tutorial, but that was with `NKFIX_CHECK=0`, so it cannot be compared directly with the `NKFIX_CHECK=1` ASM 1,350.6 ms above (a direct comparison would make it look only 0.8% slower): correcting by the ~+33 ms per step of `NKFIX_CHECK=1` (measured on B0), the branch is ~3% slower than ASM (estimated, not measured), consistent with the 1.027 of the flyr29 arm, which is also r16 + r29.
7. **The top e2e lever is actually GEMM (hipBLASLt).** The first problem was a misplaced library path: 402 files sat in a `library/gfx1250/` subdirectory; after setting `HIPBLASLT_TENSILE_LIBPATH`, A0-VR at 32 layers went from 244 to 2,027 tok/s (8.3×). The second problem was that the NN (dgrad) and wgrad layouts lacked pure-bf16 tuned libraries and fell back to the GEMV tile `MT32x16x32`, at only 50–80 TF/s, with GEMM taking 94–96% of the step. nkfix (rewriting the operand layout of `aten::mm` with `TorchDispatchMode` so that it hits `MT256x256x128`) took A0-VR at 8 layers from 6,128 to 37,746 tok/s (6.16×) and the B0 32-layer ASM arm from 2,016–2,022 to 20,718 tok/s (10.3×). Only once GEMM was fixed did ASM/FlyDSL attention account for ~15–22% of the step, so that op-level improvements became visible in e2e. The cost: 21% of the nkfix runs on A0 produced NaN (8/39 vs 0/35, p=0.004), so numbers such as 49,878, 11,608/5.85×, 38,043/6.21× and +14.40% were retracted; the B0 rewrite ran 8 times, 486 steps, with 0 NaNs. `TORCH_BLAS_PREFER_HIPBLASLT` is silently overridden in at least three places; the host hipBLASLt libraries are associated with several NaN and wedge incidents, so since 10-02 (h85) only the image's libraries are used.
8. **Wedges and AC power cycles are the largest time cost.** Over 09-11..10-02, A0 went through 27 manual power cycles or reboots because of wedges ((d) d.1.4, deduplicated across sources; 25 if the two on 09-11 were warm reboots; 28 counting the ineffective AC cycle during the 09-29 driver/firmware mismatch), 9 of them on 09-16 alone; 82% of the wall-clock time of the five rounds on 09-21..22 went to wedges and waiting for AC. By the first dmesg line they fall into three classes: class A is the on-card fp32 Tensile reference GEMM going out of bounds, class B starts with an `INVALIDATE_TLBS` timeout, and class C is a fault storm set off by an out-of-bounds read in a candidate kernel. All four precursor discriminants proposed over time were falsified; the conclusion is that there is no usable predictor, so alerts are raised only on the wedge itself. The triggers actually located and eliminated: the on-card fp32 reference GEMM (refcache, h50), running several shapes in one process (now one shape per process), autotune inside training steps (now an offline table), inductor autotune caused by `converters: []`, PC sampling (permanently disabled), new kernels going straight to prod, and the host hipBLASLt libraries (h85: toy first, then prod; compile-only for reading VGPRs; image libraries only). Also, risk is priced per startup (startup-phase failures on 09-16: 3/14, i.e., 21%). Power cuts also truncated git objects twice (6 and 255 objects), which is where the rule "push at the end of every round" comes from.
9. **The rulers and criteria themselves rewrote the conclusions several times.** Per-call interleaved timing systematically biased the FlyDSL/ASM ratio low (fwd by ~25%, bwd by ~3.4%); after switching to blocked timing, two false wins were retracted; conclusions can be opposite between randn and real data, and between the op clock and the training operating point (~1.28 GHz after GEMM bursts); the framework's criteria went wrong three times (A0 r15 promoted on an arithmetic mean, B0 r27 misjudged as target_met by geomean, A0 r24 a false positive from the fast median), each corrected by hand. After A0's 09-29 firmware reflash (VBIOS 630A→700E, SMU 125.7.1→125.12.0, with the driver changed at the same time from 7.1.1-2397345 to 7.1.0-2411946), the same code ran 1.24–1.67× faster at op level, and all A0 absolute numbers from before 09-29 are void. The e2e 1.44× (1,947→1,351 ms) cited by the tutorial is not purely the effect of the reflash: the 1,947 ms comes from the 09-28 batch of runs that was later voided as a whole (a0_p3a); when that process first loaded the FlyDSL fwd tree at step 2, the tree's `_env.py` repointed `HIPBLASLT_TENSILE_LIBPATH` to the host library (whether the subsequent GEMMs were affected is unproven); the 1,351 ms was measured on 10-02, and in between the driver was changed again on 10-01, to 7.1.0-2412954.
10. **Unfinished work and next steps.** ① fwd: still ~8% slower at op level, 1.349× at the training operating point, ~9 ms per step in e2e. ② s6 has not yet been ported into the product branch `dev/lhz/llama31_attn_opt` (the branch has r29, 1.235× of ASM at op level); the PR has not been opened yet, and the 1.032 cited in `PR_BODY.md` is an old cross-machine number from B0 09-28 that needs updating. ③ The gb scoring ruler for the training operating point is designed but not installed; the fwd job has been restored from the B0 backup but not launched; both need user approval. ④ The 5-GEMM fused version w4f is limited by whole-chip atomic throughput and is paused. ⑤ Unexplained phenomena: in e2e, ASM is faster than either op ruler measured it (suspected: the inputs are hot in MALL; not measured); the causes of class-B wedges and of startup-phase MES faults; the root cause of the nkfix NaNs on A0. Several skill documents also need correcting; see the end of section (d).
