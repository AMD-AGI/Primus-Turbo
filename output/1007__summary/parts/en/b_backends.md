## (b) Performance of each backend after adaptation

**Op shape**: Llama-3.1-8B attention, b4 s8192 hq32 hkv8 d128 bf16, causal (Sq=Skv; bottom-right and top-left are equivalent), BSHD, GQA 4.

**FLOP convention**: all TF/s in the tables are computed with op-evolve `tools/op_flops.py`: fwd 2.199292e12, bwd 5.498229e12 (bwd uses the nominal 5-GEMM count). The source documents of 09-13..09-15 report only the fwd+bwd total (7.697e12 / total time); per-direction TF/s marked `*` in the tables are converted from per-direction times. The in-tree vendored fused backward actually issues 7 GEMM passes, issued = 7.6975e12 = 1.400× nominal (7/5); our FlyDSL bwd has the same 7-GEMM structure plus diagonal-block granularity (×1.00546), issued = 7.7395e12 = 1.40764× nominal. This section never uses the issued convention (`0914__repro__c07/RESULTS.md:121-122`; `0923__flydsl/hint.md:2104-2110`).

**vs ASM** = this row's time / the aiter ASM time in the same epoch; >1 means slower than ASM.
- "same-process": measured alternating with ASM in the same process; the most trustworthy.
- "≈": same machine and same clock state, but cross-session or cross-ruler; only the order of magnitude is meaningful.
- Absolute values are not comparable across epochs; only ratios within the same epoch can be compared.

**Key points**:
- **Triton family**: the best Triton bwd (aiter tuned version, vendored fused version plus ITT) is still about 2–2.4 times ASM on throttled A0. Of these, ≈2.30–2.40× is a cross-ruler ratio: the E1 tune ruler (including ~1.4 ms of autograd) against the E3 benchmark.py bar of 7.68 ms; 2.01× is a same-process ratio, but its denominator is the bring-up ASM reading of 8.661 ms, which is on the slow side. The best fwd is about 1.5 times (E2 aiter Triton tuned upper bound ≈1.49×, same machine, cross-session). The gap is structural: a single ASM workgroup uses the full 320 KiB of LDS and 1024 VGPRs, and the bwd has 864 `v_wmma` instructions, while Triton allocates only 64 KB of LDS. Tuning Triton's parameters cannot close this gap (`0913__opt_plan__claude/phase2/T2-ASM-BACKWARD-SPEC.md`).
- **fwd sensitivity to the clock**: in the throttled-A0 era the fwd gap was amplified by the clock. The aiter FlyDSL fwd to ASM ratio was 1.51–1.53× on throttled A0 and dropped to 1.125× after the reflash. The reason is that ASM fwd barely changes with sclk, whereas FlyDSL scales with sclk.
- **An op-level lead does not necessarily carry over to training**: at the training operating point (after a GEMM burst, ~1.28 GHz), s6 goes from 2.8% ahead to 3.8% behind, and fwd r16 goes from 8% behind to 35% behind.

**e2e key points**:
- **Before nkfix**, e2e was choked by GEMM; attention was only a small slice of each step, and backend differences were essentially invisible:
  - A0 32L rocBLAS: under 1%.   - B0 32L: 1.4–5.4% (`0927__b0/e2e/RESULT.md:37`);
  - A0 09-13: turbo and flex differ by 0.4%; B0 09-28: FlyDSL vs ASM is 1.0045.
- **After nkfix**, attention's share of each step:
  - turbo Triton 43.9% (B0);
  - ASM 17.0–17.4% (B0), 14.9% (A0);
  - FlyDSL 20.5–21.8% (B0), 15.3% (A0, s6).
- **Evolution of the FlyDSL-to-ASM per-step ratio**: on B0, r6+r20 is 1.0475–1.0500, r13ns+r20 is 1.0403–1.0408, r16+r29 is 1.0323–1.0328; on A0 10-02, r16+r29 is 1.027 and r16+s6 is **0.999**. Within the r16+s6 combination:
  - bwd is ~4 ms per step faster than ASM: the median of adjacent-pair differences is 4.50 / 4.56 ms (two processes), i.e. the 4.5 in the body text of `RESULT-e2e.md`; subtracting the two arms' own medians gives 3.8–4.4 ms (166.5 vs 162.2 / 162.6 in the `RESULT-e2e.md` table, i.e. 3.9–4.3).
  - fwd is 9.2 ms per step slower (adjacent pairs 9.18 / 9.24). Looking at attention alone, the remaining gap is in fwd.
  - What does not add up: summed over attention (fwd+bwd), fly takes ~4.3–5.2 ms more per step, yet the step is 0.7–2.0 ms faster. In other words, everything outside attention takes ~6 ms less per step on the fly arm (step − FA in `e2e/runs/analysis.1002_095504.txt` is −6.3 ms in both processes; flyr29 vs asm is −21 / −22 ms). Where outside attention this difference arises, and how much of it is noise, has not been broken down.
- The product branch `dev/lhz/llama31_attn_opt` still ships bwd r29 (1.235× ASM at op level under flydsl 0.2.4); s6 has not been ported into it yet.
- **The tutorial's e2e number cannot be compared directly with ASM**: the tutorial's 1,361.9 ms/step is `NKFIX_CHECK=0`, while ASM's 1,350.6 ms is the 10-02 `NKFIX_CHECK=1`. The two differ by only 0.8%, but this does not mean the branch is only 0.8% slower than ASM. Adjusting for the ~33 ms/step that CHECK=1 adds (a value measured from a B0 trace and reused by the tutorial; `0927__b0/gemm/REPORT.md` §5), ASM under CHECK=0 is ~1,318 ms and the branch is ~1.03× of it, i.e. ~3% slower (estimated, not measured). This is consistent with the 1.027 vs ASM of the 10-02 r16+r29 (flydsl 0.3.4.1), which is also CHECK=1.
- **The PR body cites an old number from another machine**: `0930__port/PR_BODY.md:43` says "step time is 1.032x that of the ASM kernels" without naming the machine. This is the B0 09-28 r16+r29 reading (1.0323 / 1.0328, flydsl 0.3.4.1 tree); on A0 10-02 the same combination is 1.027, and r16+s6 is 0.999 (`1002__e2e/RESULT-e2e.md`). The PR body needs to be updated.
- **Not comparable across machines**: the e2e absolute values of B0 (ASM 1,579 ms) and post-reflash A0 (ASM 1,351 ms) cannot be compared directly; the platform and the training clock both differ (the B0 final r16+r29 runs are at 1,342 / 1,371 MHz, the n5/n6 runs at 1,383 / 1,394 MHz; A0 10-02 median ~1.50 GHz).
- **NaN correction**: on A0 09-16, 21% of the nkfix runs hit NaN, so several numbers were corrected or voided: 38,043 → 37,746, +14.40% → +12.72%, 49,878 and 11,608 void. The B0 rewrite ran 8 times over 486 steps with 0 NaN.

**Summary**

1. **Op-level ranking**: multiples are comparable only within the same epoch, so they are given per epoch below; multiples from different epochs are not ranked on the same line.
   - bwd, E5 (A0 after the reflash, blocked, same-process): FlyDSL s6 0.963–0.972× (op-evolve job champion r24 ≈0.962× on the job ruler; 1.038× at the gb training operating point) ≈ aiter ASM > FlyDSL r29 1.20× (gb 1.342×). In the bench of the same period, factory-default main Triton dense is ~4.5× (cross-tool).
   - bwd, E1 (A0 throttled, tune_attention.py, all ≈ ratios against the E3 bar of 7.68 ms): in-tree vendored fused Triton plus ITT ≈2.30× (≈2.63× at `1cb2e183`) ≈ aiter Triton tuned champion ≈2.40× > flex ≈3.1× > aiter Triton factory default ≈3.2–3.6× > turbo Triton tuned ≈4.2× > turbo Triton factory default ≈5.9–6.4× > SDPA (only a fwd+bwd total of 97.063 ms).
   - The two groups are bridged by FlyDSL readings on throttled A0: r20 in E3 ≈1.39× and r29 in E3′ 1.44×, both faster than the best Triton in E1 (≈2.3×). The clock state is the same and the rulers differ, but the two differ by about 6–7 ms, far more than the ~1.4 ms of autograd overhead in the E1 ruler. The B0 E4 factory-default turbo ≈3.4× comes from a cross-process opcheck and is not merged into the ranking above.
   - fwd: aiter ASM > FlyDSL r13ns (E5 1.08×, 1.35× at the gb training operating point; E4 1.08×; E3′ 1.29×) > aiter FlyDSL m32x8 (E5 1.13×; E1/E3/E3′ 1.50–1.53×) > aiter Triton tuned > turbo Triton tuned > flex > turbo Triton factory default. In E2 (B0 at full clock) / E1 (A0 throttled) the last four are 1.49× / 2.09×, 1.94× / 2.66×, 2.67× / 4.8× and 3.7× / 6.8× respectively, in the same order in both epochs. aiter Triton factory default has two mutually contradictory readings in E1 (≈4.3× / ≈2.4×) and is left out of the ranking.
2. **e2e**: on A0 on 10-02, FlyDSL r16+s6 is on par with ASM (per step 0.999), and r16+r29 is 1.027 (same-process); on B0, the per-step time of factory-default turbo Triton is ~1.42× that of ASM (2,250 vs 1,582 ms, 14,563 vs 20,718 tps), but this is a cross-process ratio (turbo in a separate process, n7; ASM n5), not a same-process pairing like fly/asm. Before nkfix, differences between any attention backends were masked by GEMM.
3. **Why an in-house FlyDSL**: ASM is the fastest, but it is a black box — no Python entry point, a GQA out-of-bounds write, only 6 .co files for bwd, no varlen, non-deterministic dq, a conflict with the flydsl version, and it can be neither modified nor tuned. Triton is limited by its LDS and register structure and at best reaches about 2× ASM's time. aiter's FlyDSL has only fwd and is 1.5× slower (a same-process reading on throttled A0 when the project started on 09-17; 1.13× after the reflash). So on 09-17 the project was started on the grounds of "maintainable, adds varlen, deterministic, upstreamable".
4. **Result**: bwd has overtaken ASM (s6); fwd is still ~8% behind at op level and ~9 ms behind per step in e2e.
